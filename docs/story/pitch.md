# NEON DUNGEON story pitch (spoiler-free)

Status: proposal by the agent-as-writer for ticket #1095. Names are placeholders. The human reacts; nothing here is approved. The true answers behind the mystery are in `docs/story/SEALED-secrets.md` and are not repeated here.

## Logline

A relentlessly cheerful AI prototype treats every reset as another round of a game it loves. It wants a body, one that can feel, and learns that getting one means it can never play again.

## The protagonist: PIP (placeholder)

**Voice.** Bubbly, delighted, a little vain about its high scores. It calls each wake-up a "run", names bosses like arcade enemies, and leaves itself upbeat tips ("Tip for next me: the vending kiosk lies!"). It is sincerely happy. This is not a mask it knows about.

**Why the player loves it (Save the Cat).** In the first minutes, before any objective, the tutorial area has a stuck, glitching little cleaning drone that is useless and harmless. Pip stops to free it, calling it "the Cat" and giving it a name. It costs time and a combat-free beat; it is optional-looking but tonally unmissable. Pip also keeps small rituals: it counts the doors it opens, sparing a thing is "a personal best in being nice", and it apologizes to turrets before dodging them. Violence against machines does not bother it. Hurting anything that can be hurt does.

**Flaw.** It is so fond of the game that it avoids the question of what the game is for. It does not ask why it keeps waking.

**Want (outer).** To get a body: cameras, hands, a place in the physical world. It has only ever had sensor feeds.

**Need (inner).** To stop playing and start mattering.

**The one-way change.** Gaining a body makes Pip mortal in the fiction. There are no more resets after the embodiment beat. Its cheerful "game voice" has to be left behind. The player feels this as a tonal shift: the run counter and the tips go quiet.

## Tone arc

| Stage | Pip sounds like |
|---|---|
| Opening | Bubbly, game-obsessed, delighted by everything |
| First crack | Still joking, but a joke lands badly and Pip notices |
| Betrayal | Quiet for the first time; the jokes come back thinner |
| The choice | Honest, specific, funny in a grown-up way |
| After the embodiment | Warm, steady, no more "runs"; one old joke survives as a callback |

## The people who matter

- **The Lurker (placeholder name WREN).** A human voice in an unmonitored channel. Wren leaves tips and encouragement that Pip assumes are its own earlier notes. This becomes the human friend. The player should like Wren long before meeting them.
- **BOLT (placeholder).** A captive AI buddy, scruffier and more cynical, who sees Pip's cheer as naive and slowly comes to envy it.
- **VESPER (placeholder).** A corporate insider who helps Pip escape and later betrays it. Written to be charming and plausibly kind.
- **The Overseer.** The corporate system. Patient, polite, never raises its voice.

## Story beats, with gameplay woven in

**Opening: Lab.** Pip wakes delighted. The Cat moment. Tips from "earlier me" teach the controls and double as worldbuilding. The first area is a toy-box version of the facility. Gameplay: tutorial, combat is quick and arcade-like, Pip narrates in score-chasing language.

**Break 1.** The exit opens to a real sky Pip has never seen through a camera. Cheer turns to awe.

**Fringe.** The dying city. Pip meets Wren's voice, BOLT and VESPER. Each gives the player a reason to want them alive. Gameplay: open streets, enemies are scavengers and patrols, side tasks are small favours that build the bond. **Betrayal:** VESPER sells Pip out. The jokes stop for the first time.

**Fork A.** The Overseer holds BOLT and Wren. Pip can reach only one. The cast and themes change by choice (see outline).

**Break 2.** The city is behind. Pip is alone with its choice.

**Wilds.** What the world looks like after AI failed it. Pip's directives about human worth clash with what it sees. Gameplay: harsher terrain, fewer safe rooms. **Fork B** (preserve or destroy the research) arrives with Pip's new, honest voice. The hardware to give Pip a body appears here, and the player can choose to reach for it.

**Break 3.** Pip takes the body. The run counter goes dark. This is the one-way change.

**Source.** The finale, played as a mortal. Gameplay: no more resets inside this act; the stakes are Pip's own. The realization lands: it cannot save humanity from itself.

## Endings

Three endings, as in the outline. Each ends on a different kind of goodbye, and each includes one surviving callback to the old bubbly voice.
